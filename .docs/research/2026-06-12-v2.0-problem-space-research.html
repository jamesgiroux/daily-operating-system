Do We Even Need This?

Problem-space research run the way a CPO would run it: five whys, behavioral evidence, the null hypothesis argued properly, GBrain actually researched, the platform-overlap audit — with "don't build" a fully valid outcome.

Method: seven independent persona streams — a discovery PM (five-whys problem validation), a behavioral analyst (14 days of audit log + the replica DB, read-only), a skeptical buy-vs-build analyst (null hypothesis + GBrain + Granola + Claude-only stacks), a principal engineer (minimal-system analysis, cold read), a category CPO (gstack office-hours + plan-ceo-review method), a corpus archaeologist (.docs/research/ + gstack work product), and a platform-overlap researcher (Claude live artifacts + Cowork scheduled tasks). Each was instructed to treat our own product docs as claims under test and was blocked from reading the v2.0 vision as authority. This document corrects its predecessor — the overnight research compendium — which was corroboration-shaped: it validated stated hypotheses instead of testing the problem space.

§3 · The null hypothesis, argued properly

"Assemble, don't build" covers Jobs 1 and 4. It cannot cover 2, 3, and 5.

The strongest honest don't-build: Granola Business ($19/mo) + Claude Enterprise Projects + Cowork scheduled tasks ≈ 60–70% of the five jobs, with the human as the trust band and ~30–60 min/week of manual context maintenance per active account. Where it breaks, it breaks on the same three gaps every time.

§4 · Platform overlap (live artifacts · scheduled tasks)

What Anthropic ate, what survives, and the parasitic option.

James's instinct confirmed: pieces of what we built now ship in Claude. Live artifacts (Apr 2026): persistent HTML dashboards re-querying MCP connectors — on-open refresh only, no push, no alerts, no sharing yet, local to one machine. Cowork scheduled tasks: a morning brief from Gmail+Calendar is demonstrably feasible — machine-awake + app-open constraints locally (cloud routines via Managed Agents lift that), in-app delivery, and cross-run memory loose enough that practitioners bolt on third-party stores. Anthropic ships a Cowork-adjacent release roughly every 3 weeks.

Platform ate itSurvives — structurally out of platform reach
On-demand meeting prep from calendar+email (adequate, not rich) · naïve auto-dashboards from connected apps · recurring morning-brief generation · work-product drafting with maintained context (Projects) Typed claim model with provenance + lifecycle (memory is synthesized prose; "dreaming" reads transcripts, not entity records) · per-claim trust bands + staleness (Claude's confidence is response-level) · correction loop that mutates specific claims · longitudinal entity threading + contradiction detection · local owned claim store (their own compliance warnings are the absence) · shareable trust-marked output · me-shaped source-reliability learning. Stable gaps on a 12-month view.

The parasitic option (researched as structurally viable): DailyOS as the MCP source feeding Claude-native rendering — live artifacts and scheduled tasks call our local MCP for trust-banded briefing JSON; Claude provides rendering, scheduling, and (when it ships) sharing. Division of labor: DailyOS owns intelligence, memory, trust; Claude owns the display surface. This is a serious alternative to building W1's document renderer at all — bounded platform risk because the core value never lives in the MCP seam. It converges with the engineering finding in §5: the briefing-delivery problem may be a rendering-channel choice, not a surface-construction program.

§5 · Engineering lens (principal-engineer cold read)

The five jobs fit in ~16k LOC. The repo holds 516k.

The decisive observation: the system already delegates all intelligence to Claude CLI sessions over PTY — 433k LOC of Rust is, at its core, a context-assembler and output-router around Claude calls. The genuinely novel ~20% (claim anatomy, trust bands, claim-files projection, composition contract) is wrapped in ~80% machinery serving surface-independence, scale, and feedback volumes that remain hypothetical.

§8 · Synthesis

The answer, and the tension it creates with last night's plan.

Do we even need this? The panel's convergent answer: the problems are real but smaller than the build, and the build's next increment was pointed at the wrong layer. What is validated: the durable, owned, accumulated context (Job 4 reframed — the thing whose loss changed behavior); the daily briefing as a liked, survivable layer (Job 1); and a trust substrate that is genuinely unique in the market (Jobs 2/3/5's residue — no personal-tier alternative exists, confirmed against GBrain, Granola, Claude, and the platform's 12-month trajectory). What is not validated: that anyone — including the builder — currently consumes what the system produces, or shares trust-banded output. The moat is real as architecture and unproven as product.

This conflicts with ADR-0139's accepted program, and the conflict should be faced rather than smoothed: the CPO lens declines to fund the v2.0 surface rebuild as the next dollar; the engineering lens says the five jobs need ~16k LOC plus the substrate; the behavioral lens pre-answers Gate 2 in the negative. What survives of last night intact: the two-gate structure (vindicated — the gates exist precisely to catch this), W0's substrate fixes (token burn and write shapes are real regardless), the deletion-as-AC discipline, and the six-primitives vision as a hypothesis about what comes after consumption is proven. What this research argues changes: before any conversion wave, run the consumption sprint (one pushed briefing, correction-by-reply, two weeks — possibly delivered through the §4 parasitic channel rather than new surface) and the one-colleague pilot. That is not a new gate — it is Gate 2 (DOS-869) sharpened from "instrument the moat" to "buy a reader," with the colleague pilot as its second arm. The urgency comes from the evidence itself — every week of building-without-a-reader extends a 19-week pattern — not from any external event. Correction log (2026-06-12, James): the first issue of this document over-weighted a single uncorroborated report (the company's possible Glean→Claude transition) into a strategic urgency driver across §6 and §8 — the exact recency-anchoring failure the claims substrate exists to prevent: weight should come from corroboration and source reliability, not from being the most recently said thing. The weighting is corrected throughout; the transition report now appears only as labeled, uncorroborated context.

James's call to make, stated plainly: accept the re-pointing (consumption sprint + colleague pilot become WI; conversion waves wait on a reader, not just a readout), or overrule with reasons the record should carry. Either way, this document is the problem-space baseline the v2.0 program should have started from.
